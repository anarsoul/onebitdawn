onebitdawn_

Make a clip pack, the alarm sounds of a onebitdawn clock, out of your own files: tracker modules, Commodore 64 and ZX Spectrum tunes, sound files. Everything happens in this browser; no file is sent anywhere.

Web installer GitHub Manual

  1. The clock's own page says which, under its forms. A clock at v0.5 plays samples of 8 bits only, and refuses a pack with a clip of 16; its module player does not have every effect of a module either. A pack made for v0.5 plays on every later firmware.

  2. Choose the sound

    What the clock plays through, which the clips are shaped for: a buzzer has no bass and wants the level pushed hard; a speaker, in the buzzer's place or on the line output's amplifier, plays the bass and wants neither.

    How loud a clip of a tracker module or a ZX Spectrum tune is made is how hard its limiter is pushed: at its loudest it flattens the peaks, which a speaker gives as clicks and overdrive under a loud tune; each step down is fewer of them and a quieter clip, the cleanest some 6 dB under the loudest. A clip with a knee in its options keeps it.

  3. Add the files

    1. Download the pack

    Sending it to the clock

    1. Open the clock's Clips page, http://<the clock's address>/clips, on the network the clock is on.
    2. Choose the file this page gave you, type the PIN the panel shows, and upload.
    3. The clock checks the pack, restarts and has the new sounds in its alarm list. An alarm keeps its sound if the new pack has a clip of the same name; if it has not, the alarm is the beeps until you choose another.

    A pack replaces the one the clock had: put everything you want to keep in it, the default clips too (their files are in the project's clips/; a clip made already goes in as it is). Up to 16 clips, 1152 KB together.

    Options

    Each kind of file is converted with settings chosen by ear on a clock, and a clip's Options field changes them: key=value, with commas between, as song=2,seconds=90.

    Tracker modules: .xm, .mod, .s3m

    The clock plays the module itself, up to 32 channels (twenty for a clock at v0.5); what a module uses that the player lacks is listed under the clip.

    highpass=300
    The bass cut's corner in Hz (100 for a speaker), and passes=1 how many times it is applied, 0 to 2.
    drive=160
    What the limiter aims a peak at, 127 being full scale; more is louder and clips.
    release=5
    How fast the limiter lets go, 1 to 15; lower is faster and louder (7 for the buzzer, 10 for a speaker).
    knee=100
    The level the limiter starts at; above the tune's peaks it is off.
    knee=auto
    The lowest knee, and so the loudest clip, at which the limiter flattens no more than 60 samples a minute at full scale: on a speaker a flat top is a click where an instrument plays alone. tops=600 allows more and is louder; tops=0 is the limiter off. The knee it found is in the clip's details. The speaker's choice above, how the clips are made, is this for every module and ZX Spectrum tune: 1650, 600 and 60.
    raise=1:2
    Play instrument 1 two octaves higher, for a bass line a buzzer cannot play.
    rate=16000
    Samples a second, 31250 by default; 16000 and 8000 are less work for the clock.
    samples=16
    A module's 16-bit samples are kept in 8 bits a sample and a sixteenth more, each predicted from the samples before it: nearly the sound of the 16 bits. A short sample, and those of a tune with more of them sounding at once than the clock has time to decode, are kept as they are; the clip's notes say so. This keeps them all as they are, at twice the bytes; samples=packed predicts them all. Either way the clip needs a firmware after v0.5, and neither is taken in a pack for v0.5.
    samples=8
    Cut them to 8 bits, which is what a pack for v0.5 gets: under a quiet instrument 8 bits hiss.
    Commodore 64 tunes: .sid

    The tune's own code is run here and the clock gets what it wrote to the SID chip, to the point where the tune loops. PSID files only.

    song=2
    Which of the file's songs, from 1.
    seconds=90
    Cut the tune here; without it the clip ends where the tune comes round.
    mute=2
    Keep the voices silent for the first frames, for a tune that clicks as it starts.
    model=8580
    The chip, 6581 or 8580, if not what the file says; system=ntsc or pal the machine.
    flat
    No limiter, a plain gain set by drive (127 is full scale; 180 is 3 dB louder and clips the peaks). A speaker's default; limiter is the buzzer's, for either.
    highpass, passes, drive, release, rate
    As a module's; for a speaker the bass cut is 150 Hz, twice.
    ZX Spectrum tunes: .pt3

    Pro Tracker 3 and Vortex Tracker II modules for one AY chip, played by the clock itself.

    restart
    A ringing alarm starts the tune over from its top, for a module whose loop is only its ending.
    machine=pentagon
    Whose clock and frame rate, pentagon or spectrum, if the tone table guesses wrong; clock=1773400 for another.
    chip=ym
    A YM2149 and not an AY-3-8910.
    flat
    The chip as it is, no limiter.
    noise=11
    The noise at so many sixteenths of its level; noise-lowpass=3000 takes its hiss.
    highpass, passes, drive, release, knee, tops, rate
    As a module's; the knee is the nearest of fifteen the clip can say.
    Sound files: .wav

    Sampled sound takes the most room: 17 KB a second, its 16-bit samples kept in about a byte each, so the partition holds a little over a minute of it. Such a clip needs a firmware after v0.5; a pack for v0.5 gets IMA ADPCM.

    seconds=20
    Keep this much, faded out at the end, from skip=5 seconds in.
    rate=8000
    Samples a second: 16000 by default, 8000 for half the room, 31250 for twice it.
    format=adpcm
    IMA ADPCM, four bits a sample: half the room, audibly rougher, and what a pack for v0.5 gets.
    format=pcm16
    The 16 bits as they are: twice the room, nothing lost; not for v0.5.
    format=pcm
    Eight bits a sample: the room of the default and not its sound; any firmware plays it.
    highpass=900
    The bass cut (100 for a speaker) and passes=2 how many times.
    drive=20
    The dB pushed into the limiter (10 for a speaker), 0 for none.

    The same converters are a command, onebitdawn-pack, in each release.
